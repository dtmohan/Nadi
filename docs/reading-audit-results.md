# KP and BNN reading audit

The changes fix presentation and scope, and remove unsupported personal claims from the BNN children reading. They do **not** establish that the underlying astrological rules predict a person's occupation or family circumstances. The [protocol](reading-audit-protocol.md), [seven-case development results](reading-audit-results.json), [four-case additional sample](reading-audit-holdout-results.json), and their linked public sources record the details. The user's personal chart was computed separately and is not stored here.

## KP scope and condition checks

The 7th-cusp harmony and opponents rules and the 11th-cusp marriage-quality rule retain their existing provisional three-of-four threshold; the words now say what the code checks. The actual matched houses remain in the evidence. The 10th-cusp employment and two children-related rules likewise now name their implemented minimum. Changing the threshold itself requires checking the relevant source, not assuming that the previous prose was a complete transcription.

The general KP comparison now uses only rules that actually address the general question. Reappointment, reinstatement, tax scrutiny, a marriage with a particular party, marital harmony, and a specific child's circumstances remain separate findings rather than becoming a verdict on whether a career, marriage or children exist. [Andrew Dutta's public KP rules](https://kpastrologylearning.com/free-kp-astrology-rules/) discuss employment and reinstatement as different questions; they do not establish that either claim is predictive. The former per-house Dutta source links returned 404, so citations now lead to the consolidated rules page. The KP tab's brief also names the specific topic and no longer tallies unlike findings as a promise or caution for an entire house. A general children reading is labelled a traditional indication, with its uncertainty.

In the additional sample, the harmony and 11th-cusp rules for [Pablo Neruda](https://www.astro.com/astro-databank/Neruda%2C_Pablo) matched **2, 5 and 11** without 7, and the opponents rule for [Garry Kasparov](https://www.astro.com/astro-databank/Kasparov%2C_Garry) and [Rita Lee](https://www.astro.com/astro-databank/Lee%2C_Rita) matched **5, 8 and 12** without 7. The new evidence and threshold wording are consistent in each. This checks implementation, not the truth of a source's prediction. All three birth times have the uncertainty described in the fixtures; Neruda's 21:00 is rounded and a different time has been published.

[Maya Angelou](https://www.astro.com/astro-databank/Angelou%2C_Maya) is an instructive counterexample: a KP children rule gives an adverse indication under her reported birth time even though [her son is documented](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/maya-angelou). The sub lord also changes under a minute later in this computation. The app now states the rule as a conditional traditional indication that cannot establish an individual outcome; it does not reinterpret the known child away or claim her reported birth time is wrong.

## Career and children readings

Career now retains its original three ranked findings and, when available above an explicitly provisional presentation threshold, exposes a finding anchored on the other Jupiter/Saturn reference. Its score and the underlying fired rules are unchanged. The extra item and headline describe alternatives rather than predicting a single job. A hard career finding that lists a profession first and a possible break second now names the break, not the profession, as the caution in a mixed headline. BNN trio wording says planets can be linked by sign without sharing a sign; the adjacent-sign extension is labelled provisional.

For the seven-case development set, the deliberately broad, predeclared word-stem check found the documented career category among visible findings in **4/7 both before and after**. The new headline brings computing into view for [Steve Wozniak](https://computerhistory.org/profile/steve-wozniak/) and [Alan Turing](https://www.turing.ac.uk/about-us), but misses the chosen music category for [Édith Piaf](https://www.universalmusic.fr/artistes/20000091849) and [A. R. Rahman](https://www.grammy.com/artists/ar-rahman/6529) and the sport category for [Garry Kasparov](https://www.chess.com/article/view/world-chess-champions). These are genuine limitations of the current displayed reading.

The four additional cases produced **1/4** matches under that same coarse check: Ada Lovelace's displayed findings mention a technical term; Kevin Mitnick, Rita Lee and Pablo Neruda's displayed findings do not mention their selected categories. “Technical” words also appear in several non-technical-labelled cases, so a long list can register a match while remaining unspecific. The samples are small, non-random and unblinded, and the public subjects were selected for known careers; do not treat these figures as accuracy estimates. The additional results were seen before the final scope and wording corrections, so they are not a clean final holdout.

The BNN children headline, details and report describe the actual Jupiter/Venus and related links as traditional interpretations. They no longer state a person's fertility, route to parenthood, timing, a child's sex or a family-size prediction. The API response no longer emits the old `sons`, `daughters`, `undecided` and `cancelled` count fields; consumers that used those fields must update. Some other schools' practitioner rules retain literal source claims and need their own review; this audit does not establish their accuracy.

An additional calculation bug was encountered on Ada Lovelace's historical London chart: a one-minute local-mean-time offset was rendered as an invalid timezone string. The display-zone formatter now produces a parseable value while retaining the exact offset for the birth instant.
