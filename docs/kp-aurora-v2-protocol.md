# Aurora mixed-house adapter v2: fixed validation protocol

This is an experimental source-informed adapter for Leena Aurora, *The Ultimate Guide to KP Astrology Techniques* (2024), supplied Kindle capture pp. 260–262, 360–365, 462–474 and 582–599. Capture-page numbers identify the supplied text and images, not necessarily printed page numbers. The book's examples, implementation and evaluation do not establish that astrology predicts an event or recovers a birth time.

This specification was fixed from the source examples before scoring the new public cases. The earlier strict adapter and its published results remain available as v1; no v1 score is overwritten.

## Rules and open interpretations

1. For an ordinary planet, its direct coordinate is the Placidus house occupied plus houses whose cusps it rules. The star and sub layers use the direct coordinates of their respective lords; they do not each recursively apply Nadi's four-step union. A node adds its sign lord and other planets in its sign and those casting a full sign aspect. Treating full sign aspects this way is an app convention because the source does not specify an exhaustive aspect/orb scheme. In the author's 1983 and 1970 charts it reproduces the printed node coordinates, including Jupiter's link to Rahu.
2. The source treats an unoccupied star, own star and mutual exchange as self-significator conditions. Where the subject or its sub is self-signifying, this adapter also promotes that planet's direct coordinates and its cusp-star/sub associations to the star layer. That uniform promotion is an app convention; the book does not define a complete mechanical algorithm for all scripts.
3. A planet in its own star uses its sub at the star layer and sub-sub at the sub layer. When star and sub are the same planet, this adapter keeps the star and uses sub-sub for the outcome. The latter generalizes a footnoted Moon/cusp instruction and the 1983 Saturn example to other planets; it is explicitly provisional.
4. When both layers contain at least one event-supporting house and the sub contains no adverse house, the stage passes. If the star also contains adverse houses, it passes **with caution** rather than discarding those houses. If the sub has both supportive and adverse houses, the stage remains mixed and unresolved. If it has adverse but no supportive house, it is marked opposed; a silent or unsupported star/sub is unresolved. These categories are a conservative app interpretation, not a complete transcription of the author's practice. In particular the book sometimes proceeds despite a mixed sub; v2 intentionally leaves that disagreement visible.
5. Required cusp stages precede major, sub and sub-sub periods. Supported-with-caution is eligible to progress; mixed, opposed or unresolved stages stop the chain, and later raw evidence cannot rescue them. Do not turn a stopped chain into a prediction that the event did not happen. Each event scores one only when every required stage passes, otherwise zero; partial stages carry no points.
6. The event profiles and favorable/adverse houses are those fixed for v1. Employment and promotion share a general job category, so this is not a specific promotion or job-change model. In particular 5/9 may describe a transition or contract in the book and are not proof of job loss. The period-lord, Sun and Moon transit positions remain descriptive, not a required trigger; the book does not supply a complete deterministic transit gate. Date-only events default to local noon. Fourth/fifth periods are not inferred from an unknown clock time.

## Worked examples

At the author's printed 1983 chart, the app reproduces the direct coordinates for Saturn, Mars, Venus, Jupiter, Rahu and Ketu, then reads the 2013 childbirth case's cusp, Saturn major, Rahu sub and Jupiter sub-sub as supported, with caution where the star includes adverse houses. The evaluation uses the source's printed planetary longitudes and stated period lords as inputs: this is a source-conformance check, not an independent birth record.

The stated 29 September 2013 event exposes a separate ephemeris sensitivity. Using the book's Moon at Gemini 20°16′52″ with Nadi's period arithmetic gives the book's Saturn/Rahu/Jupiter path at noon. Recomputing the Moon from the supplied birth date/time with Nadi's KP ayanamsa gives approximately Gemini 20°17′57″ and Saturn/Rahu/Saturn on that date. The 65-arcsecond difference crosses a period boundary; the source does not establish enough calculation settings to determine why. Do not hide or correct either result by choosing the one that fits.

The separate 1988 marriage chart's Rahu and Ketu direct coordinates also reproduce the author's listed numbers. Its seventh-cusp script is supported with caution. The author proceeds through a mixed sub during the running major period; this v2 intentionally reports the stage unresolved and does not claim full source conformity there. Her 2022 job example also treats a mixed sub contextually; v2 does not resolve it automatically.

## Fresh-case evaluation

- Select public subjects not used in v1 or in earlier project validation fixtures, with verifiable birth reports, dated first marriages or children's births and independently linked event sources. Records may be rounded or disputed. Do not choose cases based on their computed outcomes.
- Sort each person's event dates and use alternating distinct dates as training and holdout. Sample birth times every two minutes from −30 to +30 minutes around each published anchor. Score the number of full chains in the training set. Report the top score and count of tied sampled points. Only when the positive top varies across the scan and a holdout exists, choose the tied top point nearest the reported anchor as a diagnostic, then assess the held-out events at that point and at the anchor. A selected point is not a validated time.
- At the reported anchor compare the same conditions at 200 reproducible random-date sets across the actual event span. Keep each event's local time and timezone. Random dates are not known non-events and need not preserve plausible ages or order. For date-only events also compare midnight and 23:59, explicitly reporting time sensitivity. Report the proportion of the sampled grid tied, not exact continuous-window coverage.
- Run the same frozen algorithm on the user's previously discussed candidate times and known events, with local clock/zone assumptions shown. Keep personal events out of repository fixtures. Do not alter the source rule or weights to favor the user's chart or any observed public case.

The new evaluator must be shared across the ordinary KP matter-and-date reading, Validate, Rectify and the corresponding random-date trials. Tests replay actual and randomly sampled events through both paths and verify that evidence matches. It stays separate from v1, the existing KP rules, and other schools. A result that is flat, misses held-out cases or matches random dates is reported as such.
