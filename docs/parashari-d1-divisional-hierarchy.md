# D1 and divisional hierarchy: research protocol

This experimental screen implements a *partial* set of natal and timing clues, not a complete promise of an event. It is separate from the earlier v1–v3 point totals and the earlier divisional-only staged experiment. Those older experiments remain visible for comparison. This screen cannot establish a birth time, and failure to satisfy a gate does not mean an event was impossible.

## Frozen rules

- For each selected favourable event, use whole-sign D1 and the relevant divisional chart: residence move D4/4, childbirth D7/5, marriage D9/7, career D10/10 and education D24/5. Other categories, and explicitly mixed or unfavourable outcomes, are unassessed. A move abroad needs the residence-move category if that is what happened; travel alone is not automatically a D4 residence event.
- D1 uses Nadi's existing partial BPHS 11.14–16 house assessment, including its documented missing avasthas and other limitations. The D1 screen requires at least one listed supportive condition and no listed contrary condition. A mixed, strained or silent result is unresolved, not an astrological denial.
- For the division, Nadi extends its existing v1 house-support convention to these five charts: a natural benefic in or fully aspecting the relevant house, or the house lord in its own or exaltation sign, supplies a supportive condition. A debilitated house lord is the one contrary indicator currently checked. Mixed or absent support is unresolved. Benefic nature is determined from the natal D1 chart; aspects use full sign-based Parashari aspects. This is a limited app adaptation, not a comprehensive assessment of a varga.
- The major, sub and third Vimshottari period lords are checked in that order. Each must have a primary link in **both** D1 and the event's relevant division: occupation or full aspect of the event house; an identity, conjunction, full aspect or the existing node-dispositor relationship to its lord or a listed significator. The listed significator itself counts as a link. Each period stage includes both natal gates and all prior period stages; a later hit cannot rescue an unresolved predecessor. This is a deliberately strict research convention, not a universal formula stated by Manley. The same period planet appearing more than once is not independent corroboration.
- To screen a birth-time window, require the D1 check for all eligible selected events. Then, in D4, D7, D9, D10 and D24 order, intersect the surviving candidates with **all** relevant events' natal division and first two period gates. Finally intersect with all events' third-period gates. If no events correspond to a division, skip it. A marriage event is not required to pass D4; earlier D4 **candidate** checks are retained. A missing event result for an otherwise eligible event cannot pass. Unassessed events are displayed separately, and the resulting windows cannot claim to explain them.
- Real and independently sampled comparison dates use the same evaluator at the same natal candidate and with the same event clock, zone and outcome. The trial's own event instant determines its period lords. The natal gates do not change across dates; the third-level comparison conditions separately on each event's preceding gates, then combines the empirical conditional rates. Random dates are not verified non-events, can violate event order or realistic age, and percentiles do not adjust for testing multiple birth times or rule variants.

The D1 house criteria are a limited implementation of BPHS 11.14–16, with the caveats in `shared/parashari-houses.ts`. Divisional mappings, primary house/lord/significator clues and the sequential funnel are informed by Paul Manley, *Birth Time Rectification* (2022), ch. 4, capture pp. 99–102 and 112–128. Manley uses qualitative judgment; this repository's numeric/boolean gates, requirement that every period link in both charts, and conservative treatment of mixed results are not asserted as his rules. D60, finer period levels, yogas, additional natal indications and transits remain outside this screen. See also P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, [*Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*, chapter 6](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf#page=74) for topic-specific divisional charts.

## Prespecified validation

Apply the frozen screen to genuinely fresh published cases not used to construct these rules or in earlier Nadi fixtures. Document the source, rating and uncertainty of each birth time and independently sourced dated events. Scan at least ±30 minutes around each reported time, retain every tied/surviving interval, and report stage counts and coverage; do not treat a reported time as ground truth. Do not tune rules after looking at results. Zero survivors must be reported as unresolved, not repaired by expanding the window or selecting the least-bad candidate. Compare held-out event dates against sampled dates with the same gates, and report selection and sample-size limitations.
