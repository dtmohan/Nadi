# ALP printed marriage passage: fresh-case check

The user-supplied photographs show the printed chapter headed “Timing the Marriage Through ALP.” They add an important source distinction: one example refers to a progressed seventh lord running as **either** dasa or bhukti, while the available online transcription describes an exact two-lord period example. The printed passage also calls for the natal and progressed seventh lords to connect with Navamsa Lagna, but does not specify every connection or clearly resolve whether that is the natal or progressed Navamsa ascendant. The [pre-run protocol](alp-photo-holdout-protocol.md), [case fixture](../scripts/fixtures/alp-photo-fresh.json) and [full results](alp-photo-fresh-results.json) preserve these distinctions.

For the test, the single progressed-lord-in-period condition is a **generalisation of one example**, not an established universal rule. The two D9 observations are a narrow same-sign implementation of an undefined connection: one checks each lord's D9 sign against the natal D9 ascendant, and the other against the progressed ALP point's D9 sign. They do not represent all possible aspects or other meanings of connection. The app does not combine these observations into a marriage score or use them as a denial. The separate BPHS 18.1 clue describes a condition of the seventh lord and spouse happiness, not proof of marriage occurrence.

The ±180–365 day shifts for the event sequence were sampled without replacement; each case had 372 eligible offsets, 200 sampled. For Tony's second wedding, the progressed seventh lord was active on **110 of 200** shifted dates as well as at the wedding. Thus that match is not a sharply timed indicator in this comparison. For Karina's wedding the pada clue was also present on 60/200 shifted dates; for Hébrard, 69/200. These are correlated comparison dates, not independently established non-events, and the counts are not probabilities of a correct birth time. The stricter KP Aurora v2 count and its shifted-date mean were zero in all three cases.

At the three reported birth anchors, neither *both* seventh lords sharing the natal D9 ascendant sign nor both sharing the progressed point's D9 sign held for any of the four weddings. Individual same-sign connections were sometimes present. This cannot disprove the printed rule because its meaning of “connection” is not defined and the two readings may not be the intended ones.

Tony Martin's birth sources differ materially: the AA secondary entry lists 22:10 in San Francisco, while a period magazine describes 10:00 AM in Oakland. The predeclared alternative changes which of his weddings has the progressed lord in a period; it does not resolve the birth-source conflict. Karina's database record reports a gender field inconsistent with its own biographical description; this study follows the description and notes the conflict. Event places and clocks are assumed as detailed in the fixture. Hébrard's civil wedding date is also reported in [her own memoir](https://fr.scribd.com/document/903585113/Elle-etait-une-fois) and [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/fr/name/nm0372905/). No original birth record was inspected.

The existing four-person cohort had already been observed before the user supplied the page, so it is **not** an independent fresh test of these added observations. In that exploratory cohort the single progressed-lord condition held for one of five weddings; the exact pair held for none. The new cohort, too, is a small retrospective convenience sample; it establishes neither the reliability of astrology nor the ability to recover a birth time.
