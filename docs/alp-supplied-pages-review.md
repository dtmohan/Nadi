# ALP volume 2, supplied pages 34–57: source review

The twelve user-supplied photographs show Dr. S. Pothuvudaimoorthy's *Akshaya Lagna Paddhati*, volume 2, pp. 34–57. The photos are not reproduced or stored in this repository. They were reviewed as source material, not as instructions to the application. The [online volume 2 transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/972735161/Jyotish-S-Pothuvudai-Moorthy-Akshaya-Lagna-Pathathi-ALP-Part-2-2021) is a secondary access path for the same material; the supplied printed page numbers govern this review.

## Framework

The cases use the progressed ALP as a temporary ascendant and read natal planets and house lords from it, alongside its natal-house position, nakshatra lord and pada's navamsa. The property and employment cases (pp. 38–41) illustrate this as a way to identify subjects and interpret a period, not merely as a separate clock added to a complete natal prediction. Conventional house and lord meanings overlap with Parashari, while the progression, ALP-specific combinations and karma-bhava interpretations are source-specific. Sharing natal facts does not establish that every Parashari interpretation or gate automatically applies to ALP.

Pages 43–44 and 48–57 associate houses **1/4/7/10** with present experience, **2/5/8/11** with past or past-life karma, and **3/6/9/12** with future aims and effort. Page 44 applies these groups to the ALP nakshatra lord's placement; it also says Rahu or Ketu as star lord activates its occupied house. These labels describe the author's karmic interpretation and are not simply calendar bins in which events are restricted to past, present or future. Nadi already computes both node activations, star-lord placement and the house groups. The book also links each bhava to a karma bhava, the fourth from it (tenth counted backwards): for example, seventh to tenth and fourth to seventh. That is a source-specific interpretation, not independent proof of a cause or a verified event gate.

The groups themselves do not supply a complete operational natal marriage-promise test. A reasonable research design is to present separately sourced natal factors as context, then ALP period-specific factors, explicitly preserving contradictions. The D9 sign of a pada is determined by the pada, so those two descriptions are not independent corroborations. No universal pass/fail gate or extra point is justified solely by a house's time-group label.

## Marriage example and unresolved moving reference

On p. 35 the author expressly presents a timing condition: the moving point's navamsa sign is to touch one of the birth lagna, its seventh, the ALP lagna or its seventh. The page also describes certain non-contact padas as unsuitable for marriage. Earlier Nadi wording treated this as merely an example and incorrectly attributed a requirement that the rest of the chart allow marriage to this passage. Both statements needed correction. This page does not specify a complete natal-promise gate.

The main Virgo-ALP/Gemini-birth example is arithmetically reproducible for its first padas: Uttara Phalguni 2 and 3 yield Capricorn and Aquarius D9, respectively, while Uttara Phalguni 4 yields Pisces D9, the seventh from Virgo. The page then lists Mrigashira 2's Virgo D9 as a further chance because Virgo was the ALP sign in the example. Yet Mrigashira 2 itself lies in **Taurus**, not Virgo. If the four signs are recalculated using the ALP at that later pada, the available targets are Taurus, Scorpio, Gemini and Sagittarius, and Virgo would not meet this narrow comparison; if the original Virgo ALP is retained, it would. The page does not clearly tell us which reference to retain. Nadi currently uses the contemporaneous ALP, so the latter reading and the whole example are unresolved rather than silently incorporated.

There are additional apparent inconsistencies on p. 34: Virgo is labelled both the fourth and sixth from a Gemini birth lagna, and an Uttarashada point is listed for a Virgo ALP even though Uttarashada falls in Sagittarius/Capricorn. These may be printing errors. They make it unwise to generalise an interpretation from the case without further clarification.

The app presents the p. 35 condition as a source claim but does not enforce it as a denial. In the [fresh photographed-passage cohort](alp-photo-fresh-review.md), the current-sign interpretation was present for two of four documented weddings at the reported birth anchors; the other two are potential counterexamples under those assumptions, not proof that all interpretations of the author's method fail. Birth times, clocks and source conflicts remain material, and the cases are a small retrospective convenience sample. This review changes explanatory wording, not the calculation or event scores; the linked results are from the earlier run, not a new independent validation.
