# Review of Leena Aurora's KP techniques book

Source: Leena Aurora, *The Ultimate Guide to KP Astrology Techniques: Star Lord & Sub Lord Decoded*, Wordly Wise Publishers, first impression 2024, ISBN 978-93-94075-29-0. The user supplied a Kindle text capture and page images. The page references below are the numbered `--- Page ... ---` markers in that supplied text and the corresponding `ebook-page-....jpg` images; the Kindle viewer's page counter is different. This document paraphrases selected material; the book is not stored in the repository. The author's astrological claims and her own extensions are not independent evidence of predictive accuracy.

## Reproducible example checks

The focused executable checks are in `scripts/kp-aurora-examples.test.ts`. They check the source's examples against Nadi's calculations, not whether a prediction occurred or a birth time is correct.

1. **Numbered table:** Aurora's number 45 is Gemini/Mercury, Mars star, Sun sub; 123 is Virgo/Mercury, Mars star, Jupiter sub (p. 621). Nadi's one-based table gives the same combinations. The book's description of the six split subdivisions and Placidus should not substitute for testing the actual subdivision boundaries or deciding how a full horary chart is constructed.
2. **Printed natal positions:** Feeding the book's printed 1st, 9th and 10th cusp longitudes and Moon longitude directly into Nadi reproduces their printed sign, star, sub and sub-sub lords (pp. 277–278). This is a subdivision arithmetic check; it is not an ephemeris check.
3. **Delhi rectification example:** At 15 January 1982, 01:42 in New Delhi, Nadi with KP ayanamsa, mean nodes, Asia/Kolkata and approximate coordinates 28.6139° N, 77.209° E reproduces the printed first, ninth and tenth cusp *lords*. It does not reproduce every longitude to the second: for the ascendant Nadi gives roughly 15°01′24″ Libra versus the printed 15°00′41″. Coordinates, ayanamsa or software conventions could contribute; the book does not specify enough to attribute the difference confidently. At the example's judgment instant, 22 August 2022 at 13:03:27, the same Delhi assumption yields the book's five main ruling planets (Mars, Saturn, Mercury, Rahu and Moon). The example does not clearly give the astrologer's judgment location, so the location, zone and node convention remain assumptions, not independently established facts.
4. **Limits in that rectification example:** The judgment chart shows Saturn retrograde, while the natal table does not. Nadi likewise computes natal Saturn direct and judgment Saturn retrograde. The author includes Saturn among the ruling planets while stating a restriction on a retrograde ruling planet unless it is retrograde in both charts; the example does not explain this apparent exception. Its first-cusp sign lord, Venus, is not among the five listed ruling planets; the text does not explicitly show how it is admitted. Judgment Ketu is in Venus's sign, which could be an inferred link under her node rules, but that inference is not shown as a completed step. These omissions make the example unsuitable as a complete specification for an automated accept/reject rule. The text also refers to a 21 June period while its judgment chart is dated 22 August; Nadi computes the same Jupiter/Saturn/Moon three-level sequence at both dates under the above assumptions, so the date mismatch alone does not show a different period sequence.
5. **Precision of the Moon example:** The book states a Moon at 29 degrees Taurus and gives Mars/Saturn/Jupiter (pp. 264–265), without minutes. Exactly 29°00′ gives Mars/Saturn/Sun in Nadi, while 29°45′ gives Mars/Saturn/Jupiter. Jupiter begins at about 29°43′07″ within the Saturn sub. An omitted minute can therefore explain the difference; the printed degree is insufficient for an exact conformance assertion, not proof that either calculation is wrong.
6. **Marriage transit example:** For the book's 10 December 2022 marriage (pp. 636–637), Nadi reproduces the stated Jupiter-in-Pisces/Saturn-star, Venus-in-Sagittarius/Ketu-star and Sun-in-Scorpio positions. The book gives the Moon in Gemini in Jupiter's star, but no time or zone. Under an explicit India-time assumption, Nadi puts the Moon in Rahu's star at noon and in Jupiter's star after about 17:30 IST. The stated Moon placement is compatible with an evening event under that assumption; an assumed noon would not establish an error. This checks transits, not the reported event, natal interpretation or the claimed period sequence.
7. **A mixed-period inconsistency:** In that same marriage case (pp. 634–635), the author proceeds on Jupiter's supportive 2/5/11 indications despite also listing 6 and 12, and asks the reader to set adverse houses aside at that step. Her earlier general discussion (pp. 261–262) makes the sub's support or denial decisive. The supplied material does not specify a general rule that reconciles all these situations. Nadi's separate experimental rule that refuses a mixed layer is therefore a strict app convention and does **not** reproduce every judgment in the worked marriage example.

## Recommended sequence

1. Write an attributed Aurora script specification using additional worked examples to resolve empty stars, own-star substitution, node agency, mixed results and the first/ninth-cusp link. Keep unresolved cases visible rather than filling them with arbitrary weights.
2. Reproduce the specified source examples and compare the resulting evidence with the existing KP paths. If the variant is implemented, make the same event evaluator available to readings, dated-event checks, rectification and the corresponding Monte Carlo controls, while retaining other authors' methods as distinguishable alternatives.
3. Before tuning, freeze the interpretation and collect fresh independently documented birth records and event dates. Compare candidate windows with held-out events and appropriately defined control dates, report ties and misses, and test sensitivity to birth and event time, location, ayanamsa and node assumptions. A worked example validates source conformance only; it does not establish predictive accuracy.
