# School-specific dispositor integration: public-case protocol

The case manifest was fixed before this run: four previously unused published charts, chosen for different birthplaces and careers, with published dates that map to the app's existing job, marriage and child topics. The manifest records birth sources, event sources, assumed zones and clocks, a conflicting event report, and rounded birth clocks. Birth certificates and the accuracy of their times were not independently verified. One event is insufficient for the application's joint comparison baseline.

1. Compare the ephemeris at each reported birth time against the source's rounded tropical Sun and Moon positions, allowing two arcminutes. This catches gross time-zone or geometry errors; it does not verify birth times.
2. At every reported chart, compare the ordinary school evaluations to selected-event and validation evidence. Check the KP sub-lord's A–D house sets and star lord against the KP frame; keep the KP ayanamsa and nodes' representation. Check Parashari's selected house frame and explicit own-lord occupation/aspect qualifiers without treating a partial condition as a promise. Check the BJ livelihood chain, ALP birth/progressed three-way context, and existing BNN, Jaimini and Prasna natal snapshots.
3. Where the baseline is available, replay the first random comparison set through the same selected-event API and require identical evidence. Recast a one-minute rectification midpoint for one career and one family chart and perform the same comparisons, including the first random set. Comparisons are random dates, not verified non-events.
4. Report the actual house clues and broad ALP groups and whether they changed on sampled comparison dates. Record results even if unhelpful or contradictory. Natal findings are constant across dates for a fixed candidate and cannot establish event timing. No new condition changes ranking or scores.

The test uses the app's current Lahiri natal and KP frames, mean nodes and whole-sign Parashari house setting unless the manifest says otherwise. It tests software and sourced rule application, not astrology's predictive validity, causation, birth-time accuracy or whether the app would choose the published time. D9 and nakshatra pada are one geometric relationship, not independent supporting votes; Prasna question-chart rules are not transplanted to a natal chart.
