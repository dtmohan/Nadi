# Rectification audit: verification and changes

This reviews the supplied audit of commit `50c156e` against `8ac60d4`. The public examples below are a reproduction of the audit, not an independent test of whether a birth time is correct. Reported times, sources and event-clock assumptions are in the existing fixtures. The [results file](rectification-audit-followup-results.json) records the pre-change and revised scans and a separate set of public examples.

## Findings and implementation

1. **KP score versus comparison.** Confirmed. The displayed `kp-events` total includes one natal-cusp point per distinct matter plus period hits; its sequence-preserving baseline measures period hits only. The panel and PDF now explicitly name the timing-only denominator, and the sort button sorts the displayed raw score rather than a different comparison quantity. Equal raw scores are not evidence of equal birth-time probability.
2. **Highlighting by maximum excess.** Confirmed. On Flore Landru, the earlier `kp-qualified` implementation highlighted seven 7/14 intervals and omitted 14/14 intervals that also met the cutoff. The revised display highlights *all* intervals reaching the same exploratory 95th mid-rank cutoff, regardless of excess; raw scores and natal stages remain visible. On the revised scan the highlighted scores include 7, 10 and 14. A cutoff is a within-candidate comparison, not a candidate-ranking test, and lower-scoring or unresolved candidates can still be highlighted. The default detail is the recorded interval, not the first highlight.
3. **Zero-score sorting.** Confirmed. A failed gate can have zero excess and sort above a positive raw score with negative excess. Sorting by the displayed raw score fixes that ordering without treating a closed gate as a superior fit. Jaimini's already-grouped table now uses the same raw-score ordering and highlights every group meeting its cutoff.
4. **ALP comparisons.** Confirmed. Each selectable whole birth second now checks the same sequence-preserving date shifts as Validate, including one-marriage scans. The matched ALP result carries the *joint* distribution of the pada condition across all eligible marriages; it is not inferred by adding separate marginal frequencies. Bands cannot be compared if eligibility or the actual list of offsets changes. Older independently drawn comparisons are retained where shown and labelled separately. The panel and report name the comparison actually used.
5. **Fragmentation.** Confirmed, but the proposed blanket removal of other cusps or non-own-star sub-sub lords would lose information. Aurora's self-significator promotion may use any cusp; the KP filter reads all the event's signified houses; and Aurora v2 also promotes a sub-sub lord when star and sub repeat, even without an own star. The narrower key retains those dependencies, the ninth-cusp example and the KP Moon-lord chain. KP and BNN tables group adjacent slices only when their displayed evidence and comparison agree, with other details attributed to a representative midpoint. Jaimini already had evidence-aware groups.
6. **Legacy API blend.** Confirmed. Deprecated `segment.score`, `segment.max` and `result.best` are no longer a sum across methods: `legacyScoreBasis` identifies `kp-events` when events are supplied, or `kp-rp-basic` otherwise. Clients should calculate the selected method using `methodScore`; the legacy fields cannot identify a correct time.
7. **Sensitivity.** A flat sampled score cannot narrow the window, but the audit's stated Moon motion of 0.011° in 30 minutes is incorrect. The Moon averages about 13.2° a day, approximately 0.275° in half an hour ([NASA](https://spacemath.gsfc.nasa.gov/SMBooks/SMBook8.pdf)); its actual motion varies. Equal shifted-date offset lists do not imply identical periods or event scores. A period boundary can change even while the Moon remains in one sign; neither a flat score nor a changed period proves a candidate.

An additional bug was discovered while checking historical cases: the ordinary KP, D81 and divisional windows previously attached a time-zone offset to a displayed local-mean-time clock, which could describe the wrong UTC instant. Their `startIso` and `endIso` now represent rounded UTC instants, while separate `startDate` and `endDate` preserve the chart's civil time basis for display. For Flore Landru's supplied longitude, the old timestamp could differ by 12 seconds. Older stored reports still use their old boundaries; recalculate them.

## Checks and limits

- Against the original scan, 214 interior midpoint samples across Cindy Crawford, Flore Landru and Manuel Rodríguez Lozano retained identical Moon-lord, KP natal/filter and Aurora v1/v2 evidence. Samples within one second of a rounded boundary were excluded; this does not prove every instant is stable. The [replay test](../scripts/rectify-key-replay.test.ts) records reference fingerprints from `8ac60d4`.
- Their ±15-minute scans changed from 76/101/68 segments to 76/96/60. KP display groups were 45/54/33; BNN groups were 1/1/2. These counts describe fragmentation, not predictive power. Wall-clock times in the result file are single local observations, not a reliable speed benchmark.
- ALP band endpoints and midpoints were checked against Validate, including an independently replayed two-marriage joint distribution and a single-event scan. Historical LMT UTC boundaries were checked against the birth-time conversion for ordinary, D81, divisional and ALP windows.
- Six further public cases from the earlier transit holdout, not from the supplied rectification audit, were run with the revised scan. They are not a new population sample, and their birth times are not independently established by this test. On this selected set, Jaimini's highest raw score covered the entire ±15-minute window in all six. A wide plateau is not evidence of minute-level precision.
- Rectification remains exploratory. The interval scan samples a ten-second grid and refines detected changes; very brief changes, finer planetary divisions and comparison-date boundaries can be missed. A shifted date is not necessarily a non-event, comparisons do not adjust for multiple candidate windows, and the figures do not establish that the methods predict events or identify a birth time.
