# Jupiter's ninth sign: exploratory case review

## What was tested

The [protocol](jupiter-ninth-research-protocol.md) records the selection and its limits.
Raman Suprajarama's 2026 *Jupiter in your Horoscope* lecture describes the fifth-house
Jupiter concern primarily as an inclination regarding children, rather than a categorical
claim of childlessness. At about 22:25–22:59 he suggests a planet ninth from Jupiter as an
exception. The transcript attributes the teaching to B. V. Raman; an independent written
source for that specific exception was not established. The lecture is not evidence of
predictive validity and does not settle whether Rahu or Ketu counts.

The screening was fixed before looking up outcomes; it is a local retrospective exercise,
not an independent preregistration. The [screen](jupiter-ninth-screen.json) retains all
18 selected public cases: six with a classical planet, six with only a node, and six with
neither in the ninth sign. It includes the actual calculated signs and the reported
birth-record links. All have Jupiter in the fifth at the published time and ±5 minutes;
six change Jupiter's house at one ±15-minute sample. The screening method is reproducible
from the protocol. C-name sampling, historical records and the small purposive sample
cannot support population conclusions.

**Result:** no case had a sufficiently explicit, verified account of the person's own
desire about having children under this limited search. The proposed exception remains
unresolved. Parenthood is recorded below only as a distinct descriptive observation;
it is not a proxy for desire, capacity, or any medical diagnosis. All unknowns remain in
the sample, and the case notes are not proof that additional evidence does not exist.
We did not add a cancellation, prediction, or rectification score.

One structural dependency also matters: if Jupiter is in the fifth whole-sign house,
the ninth sign from Jupiter is necessarily the rising sign. Having a planet ninth from
Jupiter and having a planet in the ascendant are therefore the *same* condition in this
particular study, not two independent corroborating findings.

## What a stronger test would need

Predefine the outcome and whether nodes count with the practitioner, collect direct or
clearly attributed statements of reproductive intentions from a larger and more diverse
cohort, independently verify clock times, and compare with matched controls and sensitivity
analyses. Do not infer intentions from parenthood or general references to family. Even
such a study could show association only; it would not establish an astrological mechanism.
