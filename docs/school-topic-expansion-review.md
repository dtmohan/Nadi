# Review of the expanded life-topic readings

The fixed sources and case-selection protocol are in [school-topic-expansion-protocol.md](school-topic-expansion-protocol.md); [the reproducible output](school-topic-expansion-results.json) records the selected topic summaries, rule IDs, dates and source labels. Run `node --import tsx scripts/eval-school-topics.ts` to reproduce the audit. The topic presentation does not create new scores; the separately documented KP education qualification changes one partial rule from negative to neutral in the regular reading.

## What was checked

All five schools expose the eleven life topics plus separate father and mother cards, subject to the existing age gates. Their coverage differs: KP's added cards select exact existing topic labels without making a general verdict from a narrow rule; Parashari and Prasna show house-level evidence with the scope identified; Jaimini labels shared areas and selected learning/spiritual findings; and ALP selects only explicitly reviewed rule IDs for the chosen date. The rule book exposes the reviewed ALP mappings. Unmapped or unfired rules are still available in the practitioner reading. The selection is presentation metadata, not a new scoring rule, and the displayed selection is not exhaustive source coverage.

ALP now uses the selected civil day at midnight in the birth-chart zone consistently in its tab, Overview and chart-tab PDF. The default day is the sky snapshot's local day, capped at a recorded date of passing. The Report page starts at its own default day. The ALP date-only view is not an event-time or location-specific transit calculation.

The Prasna Marga house walk and report here read the birth chart. The separate question screen reads the app's stored sky snapshot using the birth coordinates and a selected or simulated Arudha. It now makes that limitation explicit: no genuine time/place of question is established by selecting an Arudha. The plain house walk avoids presenting traditional statements about health, parent or child outcomes as facts; practitioner wording remains available for adult charts. The app has not been validated on authentic recorded horary questions.

## Four fresh published cases

These cases were chosen before inspecting the new output, using reported AA birth records and independent institutional accounts of work. Certificates are stronger provenance than unrecorded estimates but are not proof of exact clock precision. The comparison is descriptive; no accuracy percentage can be inferred from four public figures and four known outcomes.

- **Stephanie Wilson**, astronaut, STS-121 launch in 2006: Parashari's selected career comparison leans cautious, Jaimini is mixed, KP is silent on the general question, and no reviewed ALP career-specific rule matched the selected day. Prasna identifies the tenth-house context but does not produce a spaceflight prediction. These are not a successful forecast of the flight. More specifically, the prior four-step KP education text said her higher studies would not reach their goal, yet [NASA records her master's degree](https://www.nasa.gov/people/stephanie-d-wilson/). At the reported time, Ketu as fourth cusp sub lord lacks house 11 in the four-step set but includes it via its sub lord Mercury in the six-step set, removing the `kp4-edu-no-11` match under that alternative. The six-step difference is not proof of a positive judgment: the source also calls for additional ninth-cusp and close-conjunction checks. Her birth time is recorded but may be imprecise.
- **Michel Devoret**, physicist, Nobel announcement in 2025: Parashari's selected career sources disagree, Jaimini is supportive, and KP is silent. ALP has a general recognition theme but no selected career-specific rule. A broad recognition theme fits an award retrospectively, but it is not specific enough to count as predicting the prize.
- **Catherine Breillat**, filmmaker, 2023 film: Parashari emphasizes caution, Jaimini is supportive, KP is silent, and no reviewed ALP career-specific rule matched the arbitrary observation day. The chosen day is not a documented premiere date. The reported birth record was corrected from an earlier conflicting 20:45 to 20:15, so sensitivity remains material.
- **Ricardo Bermudez Garcia**, Olympic volleyball player, 2004 final: Parashari's selected career sources disagree, Jaimini is supportive, and KP is silent. ALP has a general standing/recognition theme but no selected career-specific rule on the final day. That broad theme does not identify an Olympic medal.

None of these four records supplies a real, independently resolved Prasna Marga question with the required instant, place and Arudha. None tests whether readers understand the new text; that requires observation or interviews with actual readers. A narrow rule may be absent from a summary despite appearing in the full school reading, and lack of an entered rule is not a negative life outcome.

## Limits exposed by the implementation

The entered ALP material covers portions of Volume 2 and class/online sources, not all four books; the source links and rule-book chapter lists expose those gaps. The Prasna reading is also partial, and its topic cards do not implement full horary judgment. In particular, the ALP p. 35 worked-example-derived `b2-marriage-navamsa-away` had been worded as a universal marriage exclusion. This change retains the computed condition but explicitly calls the generalization provisional; a non-match alone cannot rule out a marriage. No weights were adjusted to fit the cases.

The KP source *Astro Secrets & KP*, Part 1, pp. 138–141, considers the fourth and ninth cusp connections and describes sub-level and close-conjunction links in its example. The app's standard four-step check is not the complete source method. The two missing-house education rules now describe their match as partial, and the seven subject-of-study lines no longer assert that two of three houses have established higher education. The incomplete `kp4-edu-no-11` match is classified as neutral so that it does not count as a negative cusp in the regular KP summary; the pattern remains visible. The topic summary reports when the existing six-step option changes an intermediate or missing-house match. This is a limitation disclosure, not an implementation of the complete six-step/conjunction judgment or a change in event-scoring weights. Topic summaries use the standard four-step comparison even when the separate KP technical table is switched to six steps.
