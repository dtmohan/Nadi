# Source-scoped event-score experiments

This protocol defines the experiments before running the new public-case cohort. Their
match counts are app-defined, not numerical formulas in the cited works, probabilities,
or evidence that an event was impossible. Keep the existing KP, Parashari and Jaimini
scores available. Do not pool different schools into a common score.

All four checks apply only to favourable recorded events of their explicitly named types.
An unprovided event clock uses local noon and an unprovided zone uses the birth zone. A
missing/withheld/custom natal frame or unsupported event is unavailable, not zero. A zero
among assessed cases means no listed condition passed, not a failed real event.

## KP: natal promise before periods

The ordinary, four-step natal KP catalogue's explicitly registered promise-role rules are
evaluated with their ordinary principal-cusp precedence. For matters with such rules, a
support-only registered indication permits checking maha-dasa, bhukti and antara in that
order. Count a stage only if every previous stage passed and its period planet has an
existing four-step matter-house link. Count at most four stages (natal plus three periods).
Mixed, strain or unresolved natal findings stop this conservative experiment; they do not
deny an event. A missing period link stops subsequent stages, while their raw links remain
visible. The 0–4 count and use of any one matter house are app conventions; they are not
the author's complete mixed-house, transit or sub-sub-lord practice.

Sources: M.P. Shanmugham, *Astro Secrets & KP*, Part 1 pp. 167–172, Part 2 p. 203 and
Part 3 p. 12; Leena Aurora, *The Ultimate Guide to KP Astrology Techniques* (2024), user-
provided Kindle capture pp. 258–262, 266–268. These sources support natal promise, running
periods and ordered interpretation, but do not prescribe this count or settle every mixed case.

## Parashari: add one qualified children clue to v3

For a favourable childbirth, add one point to the existing v3 eight-point experiment when
the ordinary selected-house-system evaluator actually produces `pa-h5-16-16` (BPHS 16.16).
This rule associates certain fifth-lord conditions with children. Missing the rule is not
a denial, and other contrary findings remain visible and do not act as a veto. Marriage and
career keep v3's eight factors because their broad natal house findings have not been
qualified as equivalent event promises. The added point can overlap v3's D1 support, making
the weighting and the mixed house-system choice provisional. The catalogue's selected house
method may differ from the whole-sign/full-aspect v3 research frame. Report both settings.
Source: [Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra 16.16](https://vedic-astro.s3.amazonaws.com/books/bhrihat_parasara_hora_shastra.pdf)
and the earlier [v3 protocol](parashari-divisional-v3-protocol.md).

## ALP: marriage-specific pada condition

For marriage only, count one match when the ordinary ALP evaluator's
`b2-marriage-navamsa-touches` is present at the event instant: the active pada's navamsa
sign is the ALP ascendant, seventh from ALP, natal ascendant or seventh from natal ascendant.
The source requires the rest of the chart to allow marriage; this single condition does not
assess that broader promise. `away` does not become a universal denial. The 0/1 count is an
app convention and is expected to remain unchanged for long intervals.
Source: S. Pothuvudaimoorthy, [*Akshaya Lagna Paddhathi*, vol. 2, p. 35](https://www.scribd.com/document/972735161/Jyotish-S-Pothuvudai-Moorthy-Akshaya-Lagna-Pathathi-ALP-Part-2-2021).

## BNN: marriage transit clue

For marriage only, count one match if transiting Jupiter is in the same sidereal sign as
natal Venus in a male chart, or natal Mars in a female chart. An unspecified gender is
unavailable because the source assigns the spouse significator by gender. Same-sign
conjunction is the app's narrow operationalisation of “touches”; other possible contacts
are not silently counted. The Venus example specifies Jupiter's second round; this clue
does not calculate that additional condition and is therefore explicitly partial. This is
one year-scale clue, not a complete BNN marriage method.
Source: N. Srinivasan Shastry, [*Brighu Naadi Sangraha*, introductory principles and Jupiter chapter](https://ashramsofindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Brighu-Naadi-Sangraha-by-N-Srinivasan-Shastry.pdf).

Existing Jaimini Chara-dasha scores already use the same dated evaluator in each path. No
new Prasna Marga event score is defined: a natal house statement is not a question chart, and
the event's date does not supply the time and place of a horary question.

## Verification and fresh cases

The same evaluator must produce actual and every sampled-date score for a chosen-date chart,
Validate and each Rectify candidate. Test outcome gates, unknown/custom inputs, source examples,
selected house system, clock/zone and exact boundary behavior. Replay comparison dates and
compare per-event and aggregate outputs. Profile the same fixed synthetic workload before and
after optimization; compare the entire old response independently of newly added fields.

After the rules and manifest are fixed, run new publicly documented birth inputs and events
not present in existing case fixtures. Record source quality, uncertain birth clocks, time
zones, correlated events and date disputes before running the code. Scan a fixed ±10-minute
window and report top tied coverage, the reported-input segment and every event's condition.
Use 200 random-date sets only where the events span at least a year; comparison dates are not
confirmed non-events. Record all misses, ties, and unassessed cases. Do not adjust the rule or
drop cases after seeing the output. The user's chart is exploratory and kept outside the repo.
