# D1 and divisional clues: descriptive protocol

The earlier strict funnel is retained solely to reproduce its four-case result. It can stop at an incomplete D1 house test before considering the D1 house lord or significator in the division, and it omits some period-lord clues in Manley's book. It must not be used to eliminate or select birth times. A mixed or absent partial condition is not a finding that the event is impossible. A later event should not erase a documented earlier contradiction, but this implementation has no comprehensive, source-validated contradiction rule; therefore no candidate is automatically excluded by this descriptive analysis.

## Fixed implementation

For the five matters already mapped in `manleyTopicFor`, show each distinct planet holding any of these roles: the D1 matter-house lord, the matter-house lord in the relevant division, and the book's natural significator(s). Show each planet's sign, whole-sign house, own/exalted/debilitated sign status, and natural relationship to its sign lord in both charts. A planet holding several roles is one planet, not independent evidence several times over. Relationship, sign dignity and house placement are separate observations, not a full measurement of planetary strength. This extends the already displayed D1-lord-in-varga evidence to D4 and D24 as well as D7, D9 and D10.

An own or exaltation sign in the division is a potentially supportive clue even when the partial D1 house reading is mixed or strained. Do not call this an automatic cancellation of debility, denial, an established promise, an assured result or a stand-alone ranking rule. Preserve the actual D1 and divisional evidence, contrary indications, event time, and uncertainty. Degree-specific strength, contextual yogas, further avasthas and transits require separate rules.

Show event-period links without requiring the old strict D1 gate. For a period lord, report the primary house, house-lord and significator links in D1 and the relevant division. In the division also report the already-calculated divisional-ascendant, natal-house-lord and secondary-house links. Count the first two period lords as having these **partial timing clues** only if each has a listed link in both chart frames; the third-level clue is counted only after those two. The two-chart requirement and the grouping of clues are Nadi's descriptive convention. For example, the D1 matter lord retains that identity in the relevant division even if it has no primary divisional house, lord or significator link. This is not a complete natal promise and does not select a birth time. The event's division is used; a marriage is not required to pass the residence division.

The chart reading, validation, rectification and reports consume the same evidence. Monte Carlo comparison reruns the same period-clue conditions at each event's own sampled date, keeping the same natal candidate, clock, zone and outcome. The third level conditions each event's trial dates on both preceding period clues. The comparison does not establish a natal promise, an event probability or birth-time accuracy; random dates are not verified non-events and may not preserve age or order.

## Sources and verification

Paul Manley, *Birth Time Rectification* (2022), capture pp. 64–67 and 132–133, gives the B.V. Raman example: Mercury is the natal fifth lord and remains relevant in D7 even though the author's listed primary D7 links are absent. His full procedure is qualitative. P.V.R. Narasimha Rao, [*Vedic Astrology: An Integrated Approach*, §§6.5–6.6](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf#page=74), discusses topic-specific divisions and dignity; [worked example 5](https://www.vedicastrologer.org/articles/vedic_astro_textbook.pdf#page=513) considers the D1 seventh lord in D9. These examples are astrological interpretations, not empirical proof and not a universal binary rescue formula.

Test the known Raman chart as a **source transcription example**, not an independent validation case. Separately examine the three newly assembled public cases in `scripts/fixtures/parashari-cross-chart-fresh.json`: at the reported birth time, compare two recorded dates with the same 200 random-date draws, and examine a third held-out event against six ±60/120/180-day controls. Preserve every source, conflicting report, assumed clock and time basis. This is an exploratory descriptive check on a tiny convenience sample, not evidence of predictive accuracy or a statistical test. Do not modify the rules to fit these results, and do not rank or narrow any birth-time window from them.
