# Parashari event-ranking experiment: fixed rule and evaluation protocol

This document records the rule before running the new public-case evaluation. It is an engineering operationalisation of cited astrological readings, not a formula claimed to appear verbatim in any source. Its five equal points are app conventions, not source-prescribed weights or probabilities. The same implementation must be used for input and held-out events, with no case-specific weights.

## Scope and rule

Score recorded marriage, childbirth, and a new job/promotion in the chart's selected ayanamsa, using whole-sign houses, full sign-based planetary aspects, and the chart's node setting. Explicitly unfavourable or mixed outcomes, career loss, business formation, and other event types are not scored. A missing event time means noon in its supplied zone, or the birth zone if none is supplied. An event before birth is not scored. Applying house-judgment rules to divisional houses and summing them are provisional app choices.

For each eligible event, check five binary factors, one point apiece:

1. **D1 support:** at least one existing, partial BPHS 11.14–16 house-support condition holds for D1 house 7 (marriage), 5 (childbirth), or 10 (career).
2. **Relevant division support:** in D9 house 7, D7 house 5, or D10 house 10, respectively, there is a natural benefic occupant or full aspect, or its lord occupies its own or exaltation sign. This is a limited extension of house judgment to the division. Natural nature is read from the birth chart, not inferred from the event. Separately show any D1 strain and divisional debilitation; do not suppress them or describe their absence as proof of a promise.
3. **Dasa link:** the natal Moon-based Vimshottari maha-dasa lord is the matter-house ruler or occupies or fully aspects the matter house, in either D1 or the relevant division.
4. **Bhukti link:** the same test for the antar-dasa lord. The finer antara is shown as context and is not scored.
5. **Double transit:** at the event instant both Jupiter and Saturn occupy or fully aspect the same D1 matter house or the natal D1 matter lord's sign. Two different targets do not qualify. This is the app's explicitly narrow reading of the double-transit descriptions.

The score ranks **observed rule matches**, not the chance that an event occurs, its quality, a precise birth time, or an infertility/no-marriage diagnosis. The sources discuss wider factors and disagree about interpretations; a difficult house can accompany a marriage, a child, or a job. The engine does not infer an event was impossible when a condition is absent. The scoring convention does not assess a full promise, avasthas, all yogas, Shadbala, alternative dashas, or all transit rules. Job loss is not inferred from the opposite of career support.

## Evaluation

Use new public cases not in the prior seven-person pilot, chosen for documented birth time and dated events from diverse locations and occupations. Record each birth-data source, rating or origin, event citation, time-zone assumptions, and any uncertainty before looking at model output. The cases are a public convenience sample, not representative of the population. Exclude any event whose category or exact calendar date cannot be substantiated.

For each person, scan ±30 minutes around the documented time. Use alternate chronological **distinct dates** as input and hold the others out; dates shared by multiple events stay together. Require two distinct dates overall, including one holdout. **Protocol change before any scores were run:** the original draft required two input dates, but otherwise documented cases with only two events would be excluded. Include those as single-input cases and report them separately; they are especially weak tests. Among maximum-score selectable input midpoints, the diagnostic representative is the one nearest the reported time; this tie-break uses the answer and must not be described as blind. Record (a) top-score duration as a fraction of the hour, (b) whether a top-score interval touches the reported time's ±5-minute band, (c) selected midpoint's offset, and (d) held-out scores at the documented time and selected midpoint. The recorded-time score must be calculated at the recorded instant, not silently replaced with the midpoint of the containing interval. Report all cases, including misses and flat scores.

Report results separately for each event category and pooled; count the proportion of one-hour windows covered by tied maxima. Compare results against a chance time selected uniformly from the same window where possible. After the initial run, the output was extended to calculate the exact fraction of each scan for which a uniform reference time's ±5-minute band would touch any top interval: merge top intervals expanded by five minutes, clipped to the window. This descriptive baseline uses the same already-computed scores; it is not a random-date event control or independent population test. A retrospective holdout may precede some input events; it is not prospective prediction. Dated-event coverage and the diagnostic representative are not independent when the scan is centred on the reported time. Small case counts, non-original birth records, rounding, selective public biographies, missing event times, and correlating events within a person limit conclusions. Do not tune the rule on these cases and then call the same cases independent validation.
