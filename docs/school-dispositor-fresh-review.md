# School-specific dispositor integration: results

The [pre-run protocol](school-dispositor-fresh-protocol.md), [case manifest](../scripts/fixtures/school-dispositor-fresh.json), [reproducible evaluator](../scripts/eval-school-dispositor-fresh.ts) and [machine-readable results](school-dispositor-fresh-results.json) describe the inputs, assumptions and complete output. Run from the repository root with `node --import tsx scripts/eval-school-dispositor-fresh.ts`.

Four previously unused public charts contribute ten dated events: Brazilian agronomist and politician Tereza Cristina (three job appointments), Swiss-born French executive Louis Schweitzer (three appointments), Swedish actress Britt Ekland (one marriage and two births), and Brazilian musician João Gilberto (one birth). There are three independent 200-set random-date comparisons; Gilberto has only one dated event and cannot use this joint baseline. Birth times come from quoted birth records and are not independently verified; several are rounded. Event times are assumed noon unless stated in the manifest. Zones and the distinction between effective dates and ceremony dates are explicitly recorded. We did not choose cases by the resulting astrological conditions.

## Engineering checks

- Tropical Sun and Moon positions agree with the published rounded positions within two arcminutes for all four charts. This checks time-zone and frame handling, not the accuracy of the reported birth clock.
- All ten dated observations agree between the selected-date reading and Validate. KP natal traces match the ordinary KP frame's cusp, sub-lord, star lord and A–D house sets, including node representation; the BJ career chain matches the ordinary computation. ALP three-way birth and progressed contexts match the ordinary evaluator. BNN, Jaimini and Prasna snapshots remain the existing mapped, gated natal findings.
- All nine first random-date samples replay identically through the selected-date API. Natal Parashari, KP and other natal context stays unchanged for each fixed birth candidate; this does not validate a timing claim. Two one-minute rectification scans (Cristina and Ekland) reproduce both the event evidence and the first comparison samples at the recast midpoint. The earlier Elis Regina public-case regression, which was **not fresh for this run**, also retained the fifth-house own-lord-aspect clue and matched the candidate and comparisons.
- Across the nine first comparison samples, the ALP progressed three-house groups differed from the event date in five and were identical in four. These are exploratory descriptive categories; one sample per event is not an estimate of predictive skill, and random comparison dates are not confirmed non-events.

## Interpretation limits

None of the ten fresh event-house observations had the newly documented own-lord occupation or aspect clue in the selected whole-sign frame. This supports the negative-path implementation check; the fresh sample cannot demonstrate whether a positive clue is useful. The synthetic tests and the previously examined Elis Regina chart cover the positive branch as software regressions only. The nine comparison replays necessarily repeat the same natal clue for a given candidate.

All five distinct chart-and-topic KP cusp chains had at least one house from the app's relevant matter set. Those are not ten independent successes: several events share the same natal cusp, and the same links appear at random dates. The more complete Aurora scripts, period and transit layers and adverse-house considerations remain separate. A sub-lord house match does not prove an event promise or identify a birth time.

The new livelihood context is one classical career-reading approach. The verse's historical examples do not map mechanically to modern job titles; Iyer and Adyar differ on whether the tenth-lord D9 chain is a fallback or a simultaneous reading. It is not a dated prediction. The actual Prasna question-chart rules still require a question and question-time frame and were not promoted to natal rules. The additions do not change event or rectification scores, and the existing rule catalogues are not fully migrated.
